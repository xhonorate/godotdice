# Deep Cut

A cooperative dice-and-stone roguelike for one to four players, built in **Godot 4.7.2** with typed GDScript and the Forward+ renderer. You are a lapidary with a bottomless mine under your workshop. Go down with a setting full of stones and a bowl of dice, fight what lives in the rock, pull raw stones out of the walls, and decide at every landing whether to ride the lift home or dig deeper where the stones are bigger. Back at the bench, every stone goes under the loupe.

The design of record is [docs/REIMAGINING.md](docs/REIMAGINING.md). This is a ground-up rebuild of the earlier RogueDice prototype: the procedural 3D stones, the poker-hand dice and the expedition loop survived; everything else was redone.

## How it plays

- **Stones** have four C's, and every step of every one of them changes what the stone does. color is what kind of thing it does (Red damage, Blue guard, Green sustain, Violet control, Gold fortune, White the hand itself) and sets the cut shape. Carat is how much: one multiplier over everything, and for an effect that cannot be a fraction — a reroll, a phantom die, a stun — how many times over it happens, with the remainder as the chance of one more. Cut is how often: every skill has five distinct rungs, no two alike, and a better cut stands on a looser one, reads more dice, or hits for more. Clarity is how pure: a Pristine stone rings the rail for double Resonance, a Flawless one for triple, hits half as hard again, and carries the skill's Flawless line; Included, Etched and Intricate stones carry one, two or three **inclusions**, the affixes that make one stone unlike another.
- **Dice** are items too: a shape, at most one pattern (even, odd, split, Gambler's, paired, stretched, shallow), any number of etched faces (wild, exploding, shiny, golden, tally, sticky, twin, doubled, and the locked and blank ones an enemy leaves behind) and at most one material, which is the only thing that colours a die and is worth half again as much on every gem of its own colour. See [the dice reference](docs/DICE.md). Upgrades and downgrades follow d2 → d3 → d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 → d16 → d20 → d24 → d30 → d40 → d50 → d60 → d100. Starting bowls stay modest; the unusual sizes come through working dice. See [the shapes and construction notes](docs/DICE_SHAPES.md).
- **Veins** never run out of rock, only of arm: an outcrop is struck as often as you like, and every few swings the next one costs another point of health. What is worth having is buried deeper than what is not — dull rock comes away in one blow, a bright seam can take four — so working a vein is a running question of one more or walk on.
- **A turn**: everyone rolls five dice, rerolls twice, locks in. Each player's rail of stones then fires gem by gem against their final hand, one step at a time on every screen. Each stone that fires adds **Resonance**; a fizzle resets it; two neighbours of one color resonate harder; the Capstone socket cashes it in as carats.
- **Enemies** reveal their movesets and ordered dice while you plan, then roll one die at a time. Every qualifying ability fires in order; combination bonuses fire once when completed. Enemy damage and debuffs affect everyone, with each player blocking individually. Hover an enemy or use its Moves button for details, its traits included; its table opens automatically while it acts, and the die it is rolling turns over its own head. See [docs/ENEMY_TURNS.md](docs/ENEMY_TURNS.md). Every mine has its own creatures, two Wardens and a final boss (64 creatures in all), and the deeper ones fight with what the shallow ones cannot: summons and escorts, burrowing, blows refracted or mirrored back, colours drunk, hits capped by Sturdy, Strength that grows through a fight, festering, scorching and burning, locked dice and dice worn down for the fight (or for good), stolen pyrite and stolen gems fired back at you, Backlash on every gem, dampened Resonance, and the Rift's remembered bosses. See [docs/BESTIARY.md](docs/BESTIARY.md). Creature models are editable scenes, baked from data recipes; see [docs/CREATURE_SCENES.md](docs/CREATURE_SCENES.md).
- **The Descent**: a shaft charted one stretch at a time. Between landings the way down is a lattice of chambers whose tunnels split and rejoin; the lantern shows two depths ahead, past that only glints, and ore lights the whole stretch. Chambers are fights, elites, veins, oddities (gambles with your stones and dice: cut a stone again, fire one in the oven, tumble it into another skill, dissolve the rock off three at once for blood, fuse two in a crucible, raise or recut or engrave a die), merchants (stones for ore, a buyer for appraised stones, and appraisals that cost more each time at the same stall), wishing wells (a stone or a purse of pyrite thrown down it is weighed into one number — half a stone's worth while it is in its rock, half again once it has been read — and that number picks a rung on a ladder that runs from nothing at all through pyrite and stones to a raw opal or a gem cut perfect and left flawless; a pittance is nearly always nothing, a thousand's worth puts the top of the ladder within reach), smithies (hammer a die a size bigger or file it a size smaller, or stamp the pattern the anvil is set for), carvers (raise one face of a die, recut it to show another face's number, or cut the etching the needles are set for) and vats (dip a die and it comes out made of something else, or melt it back to plain numbers). A merchant keeps one die for each player as well as its stones, in a size that player already carries; a bought die is swapped for one of the same number of faces, so five go down and five come up. Every stretch holds at least one merchant and one smithy or carver. A landing every fourth depth: the fire, the workbench, the well and the lift cage stand in the room together and any of the four may be walked up to first — but a landing asks one question at a time, so the moment a respite is taken (rest for 30% of their health, one free appraisal, or something precious down the well) the cage is behind you and the party walks up to the mouths to choose a way down. The winch wants paying to haul the cage up, and it wants more the further down it has to reach: so much a depth for every body riding, out of the party's pooled pyrite, so anyone's full pocket can cover anyone's empty one. A Warden's hall keeps its cage, because riding up with a hoard is what the hall is for. Each mine has two Wardens and a final boss on its bottom floor (the Quarry: 8, 12 and 16). The boss's hall is the one hall with a cage: ride up and the mine is conquered, or push on into the top of the next mine with everything you carry, nothing banked. A Warden's hoard is three stones still in their rock, the last of them an opal: a colour and a size class on each pedestal and nothing else, so the choice is three gambles and the loupe settles it afterwards.
- **The mines** lie one under another: the Quarry, the Seeps, the Glass Veins, the Warrens, the Furnace, the Geode, and the endless Rift below them all. Each is sealed until the boss of the mine above it falls, each breeds its creatures far tougher than the last, each holds its stones to a carat band (the Quarry gives up nothing over 7), each has its own batch of skills found first in its rock, and each has a lapidary to meet at its first Warden. Every Warden passed opens onto a deeper stretch of the same rock. See [docs/MINES.md](docs/MINES.md).
- **Luck** is the one number the rock rolls against: the mine, plus how deep into it the party has gone (up to a cap), plus whatever is handing the stone over, all of it inside the mine's carat band. The Quarry rolls a shade below the pack's written weights and every mine under it rolls further above them, so near the surface a stone is likely Poor or Fair and Clear, and further down Fine and Perfect grow common and both ends of the clarity ladder open up — Pristine and Flawless one way, Etched and Intricate the other, all four of them prizes. **Nothing anywhere makes a kept stone truer for the asking.** A wheel or an oven draws a stone's Cut or its Clarity again from the table its stage rolls on, better or worse, and a new Clarity brings new inclusions with it; carat moves only where something is given up for it: the Crucible, which pays for it with a second stone, and the Grubstake, whose stakes (carat, Cut and Clarity alike) are lent to the run's copy of the rail and never reach the vault.
- **The bench** is open whenever no fight is on: from the Bench button, the haul count, or B. Gems on one tab (the rail above, the haul below), the five dice on the other; drag to set, reorder and take out, or click for the same as buttons. A socket keeps to its colour, but two stones of one skill may both be set: down the mine they are two different stones, cut and weighed differently, and which of them is worth a socket is the player's question. Nothing in the mine is worked on through a list of names: a room's card that wants one of your stones or one of your dice gives the dock over to those things themselves, drawn large where it stood, and the one to work on is clicked; a die's face, an inclusion or a measure of ore is a row of marks.
- **Void** is a rare inclusion: the gem takes no socket. It **rides** one instead, any socket of any color, beside whatever is set there, and fires right after that socket's own gem; any number can ride one socket, in a line, and the line's order is the order they fire. A broken blue ring marks a rider, drawn small under its socket, so the rail never grows and never scrolls; drag a Void gem onto a socket to ride it, onto another rider to fire ahead of it, or into the bag to take it out. Void also makes it **Fragile**: it cannot be sold or kept in the vault. Revealed fragile gems shatter when a run ends, including a fall or an abandoned run. Unopened stones can come home, but revealing Void under the workshop loupe shatters them immediately; selling one rough also yields only shards, never gold.
- **The first non-fragile stone of a skill to come home is never sold.** There is nothing to weigh it against, so it goes into the vault the moment the loupe says what it is. Only a second one of that skill is a choice between two.
- **Co-op** is the baseline. The host runs the rules and streams every step as an event plus a state patch; guests mirror and animate. Solo play is the host talking to nobody.

## Layout

| Where                    | What                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sim/`                   | The rules, pure and headless: dice, hands, patterns, the rule language, stones, the forge, the battle step machine, creatures, the descent, oddities, the profile, the state patch |
| `content/deep_cut.json`  | Every skill, inclusion, die, setting, creature, mine and oddity, as data                                                                                                           |
| `net/`                   | The host/guest session and the ENet and Steam transports                                                                                                                           |
| `profile/`               | Atomic saves for profile, settings and run checkpoints                                                                                                                             |
| `view/`                  | The screens: app shell and UI kit, workshop tabs and the mine map, the run pages and shaft map, the first-person battle, stone cards, procedural stones and dice, and editable creature scenes        |
| `view/battle/`           | The battle chamber: biomes by mine and depth, the low-poly room builder, effects, the camera rig, lens flares and the screen pass                                                  |
| `view/audio/`            | The sound: a synthesiser, the bank of recipes every sound is written from, and the mixer the screens call                                                                          |
| `view/gems/thumbs.gd`    | The thumbnail service: stones and dice are rendered once and shown as textures; only the stone a page is about is live 3D                                                          |
| `view/gems/appraisal.gd` | The appraisal: a raw stone worked out of its rock and read out, and the sheet that sets a found stone beside the kept one                                                          |
| `tools/creature_recipes/` | The data recipes every baked creature model is built from, one file a mine; `tools/creature_bake.gd` bakes them into `view/creatures/scenes/` |
| `tests/`                 | Headless suites run by `tools/run_checks.py`                                                                                                                                       |
| `tools/data-browser/`    | The balance browser: a local web page that reads `content/deep_cut.json`, ports the rules in `sim/` to JavaScript, and charts every skill's fire rate and expected effect by Cut against a chosen bowl, stone luck by depth, creature turns and encounters, chamber odds, lapidaries and inclusions. Its pictures are the game's own renders, made by `tools/browser_assets.gd` into `tools/data-browser/assets/` |

## Status

The look (September 19, 2026): every screen was redone around icons and pictures rather than text. Fights happen in one of seven procedural low-poly biomes (galleries, seeps, crystal veins, mycelium, magma seam, geode heart, the Rift) chosen by depth, with Warden and elite variants, volumetric fog and ground mist, flickering and surging lights, lens flares, drifting particles, and a camera that breathes, shakes, punches and leans. Every gem that fires throws a bolt of its color at its target; blows land with sparks, shockwaves, shards and numbers. The run has a shaft map, the workshop a cross-section map of the mines. Stones and dice in lists are cached thumbnails, which took the vault from a three-second stall to about 40 ms to open. The battle drops its most expensive effects on its own if the frame rate stays under 40.

The lantern map (September 19, 2026): each stretch between landings is charted as a branching lattice, two mouths wide at the top and a mouth wider each depth, every chamber leading to the two nearest below it. The map panel draws it with the way taken, the ways on (clickable, hover to trace where they lead), and fog past the lantern's reach with glints for what is hidden: eyes for something hostile, a sparkle for something glittering, a question for something strange. Tunnel cards show what each way leads to next. Esc or the gear in the corner opens the menu: resume (a solo dig pauses), settings (fullscreen, vsync, graphics quality, screen shake, fewer flashes, fight speed), controls, abandon the expedition (the host only; it counts as a fall), leave the party, quit.

The sound (September 21, 2026): there is not one audio file in the project. Every sound is written sample by sample by `view/audio/synth.gd` — struck bells, filtered noise, swept tones, plucked strings — and the 69 recipes in `sound_bank.gd` are baked into streams on a worker thread while the workshop is on screen. The palette is underground: the interface is wood and small stones, each stone color rings as its own kind of crystal, rock is noise, metal is the lift and the lock, and creatures shatter. The mixer pools voices on its own bus, pans anything with a place on screen, will not start the same sound twice inside a few milliseconds, and wobbles the pitch of anything physical. The appraisal is the slot machine it was designed as: light, then a chord per grade, and a Star has a sound of its own. Master and effects volume are on the settings page.

Right-click for the close look: a stone or a die anywhere it is shown, a creature or its moveset, and a stall's goods or a Warden's hoard where they stand in the room: a large live model and everything about it (a stone's trigger ladder and four C's, a die's faces, a creature's moves and trick). In the vault, seen-but-unowned skills show only an icon and a short summary in the right panel; full inspection is available for owned stones. Every buff and debuff (poison, stun, curse, block, buried or clouded sockets, stolen dice, extra rerolls, Sparkle, a Shrine's blessing, a creature's trick, enrage) is a chip with its own mark and explanation. A gem raised mid-fight is said on the gem instead of by a chip: it grows to the carat it now counts as, and each raised rank gets its own mark and number stacked in the socket's corner, with the skill that gave it named in the close look. A chamber's results stay on screen until the player moves on: the rock with every find in it after a vein, the spoils after a fight, the outcome of an oddity.

The mine in 3D (September 21, 2026): the run no longer leaves the first-person view. The party walks from room to room down real tunnels that bend and fall, the rock and the air turning from one biome to the next on the way; the ways on are mouths in each room's far wall, lit with the color of what waits and marked on a plaque, and the choice is made by clicking one. A fight seals the mouths with a rockfall and crumbles them when it is won; creatures shed ore and rough stones as they die, and on the settle those crack open into what was really won and fly up into the ore count and the bag. A click jumps a walk straight to the next room. The chart of the stretch folds out from the strip (M). Every other room is worked in the room too: a vein is blocks of glinting rock strewn across the floor, each struck with a pick swung in from the edge of the view; a stall is a counter of real stones to drag onto the rail or into the bag, with scales to sell on and a lens to appraise under; a smithy's anvil and a carver's bench stand in their rooms with their work laid out as cards; a landing has a campfire, a workbench and a wishing well for its respites, the lift cage as the way up and the mouths as the ways down; a Warden's hoard turns on three pedestals; salvage is dice thrown on the floor; and a run that extracts ends riding the cage up the shaft into the light. Outside a fight the rail, the dice and a bag drawer stay along the bottom. See [docs/DESCENT_3D.md](docs/DESCENT_3D.md).

The appraisal (September 22, 2026): a raw stone is still half-buried in its rock wherever it is drawn, so only a window of it shows, and it is drawn at the size of its class rather than its own. It says only its color and a size class (Tiny 1–4 carats, Small 5–9, Medium 10–14, Large 15–19, Huge 20+), never its carat, and never whether anything is frozen inside it. Appraising it is a ceremony (`view/gems/appraisal.gd`): the rock comes off a chunk at a time with the stone turning under the lamp, the last and largest in a flash and a lens flare, then its name, carat, cut and clarity are read out one at a time, any inclusions after them one by one, and last its grade and worth. At home the stone is set line against line beside the one of its skill already in the vault, with arrows for which is higher, and the choice is which of the two to keep — unless it is the first of its skill, which is kept without asking; down the mine it is the bag, an empty socket or the merchant's scales. A click or Space skips to the end.

The bestiary (October 2, 2026): every mine got its own roster, Wardens and final boss from the owner's bestiary page, 53 creatures and 16 mechanics in all, with the Drill rebuilt as a rig on treads. The deeper creatures summon, burrow, adapt to colours, cap hits, fester, corrode, scorch, lock and invert dice, steal pyrite and gems, punish long chains and unmake Resonance; the Rift's Wardens are the bosses above it remembered with one trait more, and every fifth is the Unmade. Creature models are baked from data recipes by a tool, and a contact-sheet tool photographs them. The design record, with the readings taken where the page was open and the questions left for the owner, is [docs/BESTIARY.md](docs/BESTIARY.md).

The owner's verdicts (October 5, 2026): the bestiary page saved a verdict and a note on every card, and the first build had read the cards but not those. All 69 are applied now: 28 approved (one with new dice), 36 changed as the notes asked, and 5 turned down and taken out (the Lens Beetle, and Adapt, Corrode, Invert and Unmake). New mechanics came with the notes: Burn (poison that block soaks), Strength, Refract and Mirror, a Kaleidoscope that drinks a colour, Sturdy (Bedrock renamed), Splits for the Remembered, a carat cap, dampened Resonance, dice worn down for the fight or for good, a Collector that fires the gems it holds, a Hollow Crown that grows its die and throws again, and the Infinite Void, which grows a d20 every action. The Glazier, the Gardener and the Assayer have new, non-human models. See [docs/BESTIARY.md](docs/BESTIARY.md) §7.

Phases 0 and 1 of the plan are complete and Phase 2 through 4 have a first playable pass: a full loop from the workshop through fights, veins, oddities, landings, Wardens, salvage and back to appraisal, solo or over LAN. The look is a first pass: procedural crystal creatures and a flat UI frame. See the status section of the design doc for what is next.
