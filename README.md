# Deep Cut

A cooperative dice-and-stone roguelike for one to four players, built in **Godot 4.7.2** with typed GDScript and the Forward+ renderer. You are a lapidary with a bottomless mine under your workshop. Go down with a setting full of stones and a bowl of dice, fight what lives in the rock, pull raw stones out of the walls, and decide at every landing whether to ride the lift home or dig deeper where the stones are bigger. Back at the bench, every stone goes under the loupe.

The design of record is [docs/REIMAGINING.md](docs/REIMAGINING.md). This is a ground-up rebuild of the earlier RogueDice prototype: the procedural 3D stones, the poker-hand dice and the expedition loop survived; everything else was redone.

```sh
/path/to/Godot --path .                      # play
python3 tools/run_checks.py                  # every headless suite (needs Godot 4.7.2)
/path/to/Godot --path . --script tools/screenshot.gd -- battle build/shot.png   # a picture of a screen
/path/to/Godot --path . --script tools/biome_gallery.gd -- build/biomes          # every battle biome, one picture each
/path/to/Godot --path . --script tools/tunnel_gallery.gd -- build/tunnels        # every biome seam, walked
/path/to/Godot --path . --script tools/sound_gallery.gd -- gem                   # hear the sound bank (a filter plays one group)
```

## How it plays

- **Stones** have four C's, and every step of every one of them changes what the stone does. color is what kind of thing it does (Red damage, Blue guard, Green sustain, Violet control, Gold fortune, White the hand itself) and sets the cut shape. Carat is how much: one multiplier over everything, and for an effect that cannot be a fraction — a reroll, a phantom die, a stun — how many times over it happens, with the remainder as the chance of one more. Cut is how often: every skill has five distinct rungs, no two alike, and a better cut stands on a looser one, reads more dice, or hits for more. Clarity is how pure: a Pristine stone rings the rail for double Resonance, a Flawless one for triple, hits half as hard again, and carries the skill's Flawless line; Included, Etched and Intricate stones carry one, two or three **inclusions**, the affixes that make one stone unlike another.
- **Dice** are items too: shapes, face sets, special faces (wild, gem, exploding, locked, mirror, blank) and engravings.
- **Veins** never run out of rock, only of arm: an outcrop is struck as often as you like, and every few swings the next one costs another point of health. What is worth having is buried deeper than what is not — dull rock comes away in one blow, a bright seam can take four — so working a vein is a running question of one more or walk on.
- **A turn**: everyone rolls five dice, rerolls twice, locks in. Each player's rail of stones then fires gem by gem against their final hand, one step at a time on every screen. Each stone that fires adds **Resonance**; a fizzle resets it; two neighbours of one color resonate harder; the Capstone socket cashes it in as carats.
- **Enemies** reveal their movesets and ordered dice while you plan, then roll one die at a time. Every qualifying ability fires in order; combination bonuses fire once when completed. Enemy damage and debuffs affect everyone, with each player blocking individually. Hover an enemy or use its Moves button for details; its table opens automatically while it acts. See [docs/ENEMY_TURNS.md](docs/ENEMY_TURNS.md).
- **The Descent**: a shaft charted one stretch at a time. Between landings the way down is a lattice of chambers whose tunnels split and rejoin; the lantern shows two depths ahead, past that only glints, and ore lights the whole stretch. Chambers are fights, elites, veins, oddities (gambles with your stones and dice: cut a stone again, fire one in the oven, tumble it into another skill, grind or engrave a die), merchants (stones for ore, a buyer for appraised stones, and appraisals that cost more each time at the same stall), wishing wells (a stone dropped in comes back as another drawn around it; ore thrown in comes back as a stone still in its rock, and the more ore the better the odds — a hundred of it is the only way anything but a Warden's hoard ever offers an opal), smithies (hammer a die a size bigger or file it a size smaller) and carvers (raise one face of a die, or recut it to show another face's number). Dice are never bought or swapped down the mine, only worked: each player comes up with the five they went down with. Every stretch holds at least one merchant and one smithy or carver. A landing every fourth depth: the fire, the workbench, the wheel and the lift cage stand in the room together and any of the four may be walked up to first — but a landing asks one question at a time, so the moment a respite is taken (rest for 30% of their health, one free appraisal, or a stone back on the wheel) the cage is behind you and the party walks up to the mouths to choose a way down. A Warden's hall keeps its cage, because riding up with a hoard is what the hall is for. A Warden guards 8, 16 and 24. Below 24 the mine is Endless.
- **Luck** is the one number the rock rolls against: the mine, plus how deep into it the party has gone (up to a cap), plus whatever is handing the stone over. The Quarry rolls a shade below the pack's written weights and every mine under it rolls further above them, so near the surface a stone is likely Poor or Fair and Clear, and further down Fine and Perfect grow common and both ends of the clarity ladder open up — Pristine and Flawless one way, Etched and Intricate the other, all four of them prizes. **Nothing anywhere makes a stone truer for the asking.** A wheel or an oven draws a stone's Cut or its Clarity again from the table its stage rolls on, better or worse, and a new Clarity brings new inclusions with it; carat never moves at all except in the Crucible, which pays for it with a second stone.
- **The bench** is open whenever no fight is on: from the Bench button, the haul count, or B. Gems on one tab (the rail above, the haul below), the five dice on the other; drag to set, reorder and take out, or click for the same as buttons. Nothing in the mine is worked on through a list of names: a room's card that wants one of your stones or one of your dice gives the dock over to those things themselves, drawn large where it stood, and the one to work on is clicked; a die's face, an inclusion or a measure of ore is a row of marks.
- **The first stone of a skill to come home is never sold.** There is nothing to weigh it against, so it goes into the vault the moment the loupe says what it is. Only a second one of that skill is a choice between two.
- **Co-op** is the baseline. The host runs the rules and streams every step as an event plus a state patch; guests mirror and animate. Solo play is the host talking to nobody.

## Status

The look (September 19, 2026): every screen was redone around icons and pictures rather than text. Fights happen in one of seven procedural low-poly biomes (galleries, seeps, crystal veins, mycelium, magma seam, geode heart, the Rift) chosen by depth, with Warden and elite variants, volumetric fog and ground mist, flickering and surging lights, lens flares, drifting particles, and a camera that breathes, shakes, punches and leans. Every gem that fires throws a bolt of its color at its target; blows land with sparks, shockwaves, shards and numbers. The run has a shaft map, the workshop a cross-section map of the mines. Stones and dice in lists are cached thumbnails, which took the vault from a three-second stall to about 40 ms to open. The battle drops its most expensive effects on its own if the frame rate stays under 40.

The lantern map (September 19, 2026): each stretch between landings is charted as a branching lattice, two mouths wide at the top and a mouth wider each depth, every chamber leading to the two nearest below it. The map panel draws it with the way taken, the ways on (clickable, hover to trace where they lead), and fog past the lantern's reach with glints for what is hidden: eyes for something hostile, a sparkle for something glittering, a question for something strange. Tunnel cards show what each way leads to next. Esc or the gear in the corner opens the menu: resume (a solo dig pauses), settings (fullscreen, vsync, graphics quality, screen shake, fewer flashes, fight speed), controls, abandon the expedition (the host only; it counts as a fall), leave the party, quit.

The sound (September 21, 2026): there is not one audio file in the project. Every sound is written sample by sample by `view/audio/synth.gd` — struck bells, filtered noise, swept tones, plucked strings — and the 69 recipes in `sound_bank.gd` are baked into streams on a worker thread while the workshop is on screen. The palette is underground: the interface is wood and small stones, each stone color rings as its own kind of crystal, rock is noise, metal is the lift and the lock, and creatures shatter. The mixer pools voices on its own bus, pans anything with a place on screen, will not start the same sound twice inside a few milliseconds, and wobbles the pitch of anything physical. The appraisal is the slot machine it was designed as: light, then a chord per grade, and a Star has a sound of its own. Master and effects volume are on the settings page.

Right-click anything at all for the close look: a stone or a die anywhere it is shown, a creature or its moveset, and a stall's goods or a Warden's hoard where they stand in the room: a large live model and everything about it (a stone's trigger ladder and four C's, a die's faces, a creature's moves and trick). Every buff and debuff (poison, stun, curse, block, buried or clouded sockets, stolen dice, extra rerolls, Sparkle, a Shrine's blessing, a creature's trick, enrage) is a chip with its own mark and explanation. A chamber's results stay on screen until the player moves on: the rock with every find in it after a vein, the spoils after a fight, the outcome of an oddity.

The mine in 3D (September 21, 2026): the run no longer leaves the first-person view. The party walks from room to room down real tunnels that bend and fall, the rock and the air turning from one biome to the next on the way; the ways on are mouths in each room's far wall, lit with the color of what waits and marked on a plaque, and the choice is made by clicking one. A fight seals the mouths with a rockfall and crumbles them when it is won; creatures shed ore and rough stones as they die, and on the settle those crack open into what was really won and fly up into the ore count and the bag. A click jumps a walk straight to the next room. The chart of the stretch folds out from the strip (M). Every other room is worked in the room too: a vein is blocks of glinting rock strewn across the floor, each struck with a pick swung in from the edge of the view; a stall is a counter of real stones to drag onto the rail or into the bag, with scales to sell on and a lens to appraise under; a smithy's anvil and a carver's bench stand in their rooms with their work laid out as cards; a landing has a campfire, a workbench and a grinding wheel for its respites, the lift cage as the way up and the mouths as the ways down; a Warden's hoard turns on three pedestals; salvage is dice thrown on the floor; and a run that extracts ends riding the cage up the shaft into the light. Outside a fight the rail, the dice and a bag drawer stay along the bottom. See [docs/DESCENT_3D.md](docs/DESCENT_3D.md).

The appraisal (September 22, 2026): a raw stone is still half-buried in its rock wherever it is drawn, so only a window of it shows, and it is drawn at the size of its class rather than its own. It says only its color and a size class (Tiny 1–4 carats, Small 5–9, Medium 10–14, Large 15–19, Huge 20+), never its carat, and never whether anything is frozen inside it. Appraising it is a ceremony (`view/gems/appraisal.gd`): the rock comes off a chunk at a time with the stone turning under the lamp, the last and largest in a flash and a lens flare, then its name, carat, cut and clarity are read out one at a time, any inclusions after them one by one, and last its grade and worth. At home the stone is set line against line beside the one of its skill already in the vault, with arrows for which is higher, and the choice is which of the two to keep — unless it is the first of its skill, which is kept without asking; down the mine it is the bag, an empty socket or the merchant's scales. A click or Space skips to the end.

Phases 0 and 1 of the plan are complete and Phase 2 through 4 have a first playable pass: a full loop from the workshop through fights, veins, oddities, landings, Wardens, salvage and back to appraisal, solo or over LAN. The look is a first pass: procedural crystal creatures and a flat UI frame. See the status section of the design doc for what is next.
