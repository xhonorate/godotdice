# Deep Cut playtest and design review — 19 October 2026

**Owner decision:** All suggestions and UI mockups in this review and its companion reports are rejected. Do not implement them. The added Soundtrack entry has been reverted; Soundtrack selection must remain inaccessible from the player menu.

The strongest parts of the game are its physical mine rooms, distinct stone shapes, and the connection between a hand of dice and a changing rail. The main usability problem is explaining consequential choices: which gems will fire, how the party pays to get home, and what committing to a respite or replacing a stone means. Small reproducible bugs have been fixed. The rejected proposals and visual concepts below are retained only as an archive.

Open the [interactive UI mockups](2026-10-19-ui-mockups.html) to compare guided and compact departure screens, a more readable battle dock, and a homecoming decision queue. These are prototypes with illustrative data, not changes to the game's interface or economy.

Preview images: [departure](assets/proposal-descend.png), [battle planning](assets/proposal-battle.png), [homecoming](assets/proposal-homecoming.png). The browser prototype passed **23 interaction checks with zero JavaScript exceptions**; six views were rendered and inspected at 1600×1200. These are archived previews of rejected designs, not an implementation plan.

## Fixes implemented

| Problem | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Menu swallowed Tab, arrows and activation keys | Standard menu controls can now be reached and operated by keyboard; focus is restored on rebuilt pages and gameplay shortcuts remain blocked behind the menu. |
| Delvers Q/E repeatedly showed the same dossier | Browsing advances from the visible dossier; looking at a delver does not equip them. |
| LAN address ignored Enter | Enter submits through the same join action as the button. |
| Equipping a tabled stone could desynchronize a trade | The offer and both acceptances are withdrawn in the published rail update. Stale acceptance validates both bags before mutation. |
| Rejected landing appraisal silently changed a trade | Refusals leave state untouched; offer withdrawal occurs only after a successful respite. |
| Disconnected trader could occupy the booth indefinitely | Their offer and consent are cleared with the presence update; their stone stays in their bag. |
| Double-firing gems understated forecast totals | Forecast totals now add every successful activation in the existing preview loop. A Chatoyance fixture changed from 17 damage / 6 Block to the correct 34 / 12; actual combat damage is unchanged. |
| No Bench could trap a depleted daily party | Only this modifier now permits descent without a respite. Rest remains forbidden; the optional lens/well and lift remain available. Ordinary landings keep their existing rules. |
| Vein waiting text included disconnected players | The waiting list now reflects connected miners. |
| Particle setup leaked shaders on shutdown | Assigning the process material before the draw mesh avoids the reproduced Godot 4.7.2 creation-order leak. Menu runs and isolated headless/rendered probes exit cleanly. Deeper-room shutdown warnings remain documented below. |
| Appraisal screenshot targets called a removed method | The tool now uses the actual tray's Read it action and inline loupe, so these targets can be captured again. |
| README taught outdated rules | The current gameplay overview now describes preserved Resonance on fizzles, the Birthstone, 35% maximum-health rests and the Hollow's name, matching the simulation and content. |

Fifteen outdated assertions were also aligned with already-authored balance changes (including fight pyrite, Crush, Miasma and capped Retain). Content values were not changed to make the tests pass. New meaningful regression cases cover the input, trade, forecast and landing defects.

## Rejected suggestions — archived, do not implement

| Priority | Finding and player impact | Proposed direction / review boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Readability at supported small windows.** A 12px logical label becomes roughly 7.7px at 1024px width. Many trigger, price, status and explanatory labels fit yet are difficult to read; dark secondary text compounds this. | Use a readable type hierarchy, fewer simultaneous secondary details and visible text labels for key icons. The three mockups show a larger-text direction. A true UI scale needs responsive layout work and checks at 1024×640; don't simply enlarge every font. |
| 1 | **Getting home is a poorly signposted budget.** 26 of 48 starter-policy runs reached an intended extraction without enough pyrite. This is partly the bots avoiding fights and their income, not proof that prices are wrong. | Show the next lift opportunity, party purse, fare and shortfall before path/spending decisions. Teach that pyrite funds the lift and does not become gold at home. Mockup 1 introduces the rule; carry its compact budget strip into the expedition. Review actual human first-run behavior before changing prices. |
| 1 | **Landing actions can commit the player unexpectedly.** Resting/reading/wishing normally closes their lift option; Warden halls have no cage. | Put the consequence on the respite action: “Rest and continue deeper.” Keep the lift and its total cost visible while choosing. This is a copy/layout proposal, not permission to change the extraction rules. |
| 1 | **Battle planning requires decoding several tiny symbols.** The scene is visually strong, but the useful information is compressed into the dock and target panel. | Mockup 2 gives each gem a readable trigger and fire/fizzle state, keeps the selected dice obvious, and labels the outcome totals. Preserve the room and models. Enemy dice are still uncertain; never present a guessed enemy outcome as guaranteed. |
| 1 | **Forecast accuracy has a larger unresolved boundary.** Fixing repeated-event totals does not make the dry forecast equivalent to the live queue. Certainty forced repeats and combinations with Chatoyance, queued replays and Void riders require a separate audit. | Before expanding forecast promises, test representative builds against actual resolution and define which quantities are exact versus conditional. Static review found Certainty's second forced activation can still be missed. This remains deferred, not claimed fixed. |
| 2 | **The first workshop asks for knowledge it hasn't taught.** Locked mines, skill icons, sockets, Birthstones, insurance and seed entry arrive together. | Mockup 1 provides an optional first-descent guide with a compact returning-player mode. Explain Ardor's benefit from unused rerolls and right-click inspection in context; avoid a long mandatory tutorial. |
| 2 | **Homecoming can become a queue before anything else.** All workshop tabs are blocked until every tray item is resolved. This slows large hauls and co-op regrouping. | Mockup 3 explores a persistent tray, comparison and reversible staged decisions. Allowing “later” or bulk confirmation changes the current progression/economy rules and needs approval. Keep replacement loss explicit; selling the incoming stone and replacing an owned stone are different decisions. |
| 2 | **Keyboard/controller access is incomplete beyond standard controls.** Portraits, custom tiles, map choices and filters still have uneven focus/input behavior. | Design a full focus route and context-specific prompts; add bindings for custom controls before promising gamepad support. Physical controller, rumble and accessibility testing are still needed. |
| 2 | **Collection filtering hides useful behavior.** Tiny color dots and modifier-key multi-select are hard to discover. | Add readable active-filter chips and Reset filters, and expose compare/inspection controls. This is a follow-up layout study; the current mockups do not implement a new Vault. |
| 2 | **Co-op state changes need better explanations.** A reset trade offer can look unexplained; a stale mining command can produce a red refusal after the party already moved. | State why an offer was withdrawn and use neutral feedback for an already-completed room action. Keep real invalid-action errors. Validate pending/acknowledgement UI under latency before changing all controls. |
| 3 | **Late-mine preparation could be more useful.** Strong generic rails fail against specialized traits; this alone is not evidence for nerfs. | Give each mine a short threat/counter summary linked to discovered bestiary entries. Test build advice with experienced players. |
| 3 | **Steam availability is discovered through errors.** The client was unavailable here, yet controls can invite an attempted Steam action. | Show transport availability and an actionable reason, retaining LAN access. Real Steam accounts/builds are required to validate invitations, overlay and relays. |

## Performance and visual observations

The workshop remained inexpensive once thumbnails were warm. A populated Vault rebuild took **38.4 ms** of synchronous UI construction in the measured fixture, which can still miss two frames at 60Hz. The room quality controls materially reduced steady-state rendering cost on this Mac:

| Scene, 240 sampled frames | Mean frame | 95th percentile | Median draw calls |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fresh Descend | 3.12 ms | 6.27 ms | 585 |
| Populated Vault | 3.23 ms | 5.81 ms | 698 |
| Quarry planning, High | 16.33 ms | 18.86 ms | 473 |
| Quarry planning, Low | 11.53 ms | 14.53 ms | 435 |
| Quarry planning, Low, outlines off | 8.73 ms | 10.41 ms | 432 |

These are measured frame intervals, including engine scheduling, not isolated GPU profiler timings. The test disables vsync, waits for warm-up and holds the battle in planning. It does not establish performance during long effect chains, streaming hitches, full parties with effects, or on other devices. Per-mine samples and screenshots are in `build/review-2026-10-19/performance/biomes_metrics.json` and adjacent images. Reproduce with `tools/review_performance.gd`; add `biomes` after the output directory for all seven mines.

Across the seven mine-family planning fixtures on High, means ranged from **14.69 to 16.51 ms**, with 95th percentiles from **16.45 to 19.88 ms**. Static engine memory remained approximately **180–183 MB** as the fixtures changed; this is not a long-session leak test. The largest sampled frame in that pass was **25.88 ms** in the Hollow.

The low-poly rooms and modeled objects already give the game a coherent identity. Keep that visual foundation. The most useful visual work is making interactable objects, target information and consequences easier to read, followed by accessible text/contrast and explicit focus/selection states. The sampled planning scenes provide no reason to replace the renderer or art pipeline. Profile actual resolution/transition spikes before undertaking a performance overhaul.

## Console findings and remaining validation

The initial baseline failed 15 stale simulation assertions plus a shader leak check. The stale expectations and reproduced particle creation-order defect have targeted fixes; the menu audit and isolated particle probes now exit cleanly. One tool-only error was found in the old appraisal screenshot entry point and repaired. **Residual shutdown warnings remain after a fight:** `build/review-2026-10-19/particle-crossroads-repeat.log` records one particle shader/RID leak and seven ObjectDB instances. Explicitly freeing the app and draining 12 frames removed the shader warning in both follow-up captures, but the seven objects remained (`particle-crossroads-clean.log` and `particle-crossroads-clean-repeat.log`). Verbose output identifies them only as `RefCounted`, each with reference count zero; their source is unresolved. All nine isolated particle-factory probes passed with both headless and actual rendering (`fx-*.log` / `fx-rendered-*.log`), and the remaining factories already use the corrected creation order. These results do not establish whether the residual objects accumulate during ordinary play; further lifetime profiling is deferred, with no speculative product change.

Steam startup messages remain because Steam is unavailable in this environment. Three ordinary live-ENet commands were safely refused when a mining room ended just before a guest's Stop command arrived; mirrors stayed equal and progression continued. Those are a feedback issue, not a synchronization failure.

Still requiring separate validation: real Steam sessions/NAT/overlay, WAN latency and packet loss, Windows and web exports, physical controller/rumble, subjective audio/mix listening, prolonged resolution stress and human onboarding. The sweep did not naturally reach every late Hollow/Rift boss or every branch of every oddity; isolated mechanics/layout suites cover more of those states. Exact limits are retained in the three detailed reviews.
