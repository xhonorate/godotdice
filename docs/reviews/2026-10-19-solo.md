# Solo gameplay review — 19 October 2026

**Owner decision:** All suggestions in this report are rejected. Do not implement them; retain this report as audit evidence.

This is a command-driven gameplay audit of the real Godot simulation, plus a rendered interaction check of the No Bench landing. It is not a claim of 162 human mouse-and-keyboard sessions. The separate menu/visual review covers presentation and input. All profiles here were in memory; no player save directory was read or written.

## Coverage and reproduction

Run with Godot **4.7.2 stable**:

```sh
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . --script tools/solo_audit.gd -- build/review-2026-10-19/solo.json
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path . --script tools/review_no_bench.gd
```

The first tool accepts an optional second argument limiting the number of runs. It uses only the public descent commands to act after constructing each starting fixture. It guards against a command loop making no progress and against an unbounded run, and applies each result to a new in-memory profile. Expected refusals from trying an unavailable oddity choice are recorded separately from audit failures.

Both the baseline and final sweeps completed **162 runs and 590 fights each**, with every run reaching a natural ending and no script errors or progress failures. The baseline took **2,221 turns**, the final sweep **2,220**: the corrected forecast changed one daily run's decisions, shortening it by a turn; all ending categories remained the same. The final JSON/log are `build/review-2026-10-19/solo.json` and `solo.log`, with the original retained as `solo-baseline.json` and `solo-baseline.log`. That is **324 completed automated runs in total**.

## Fixed: repeated gems understated the combat forecast

**Player-visible problem:** a Chatoyance gem says it fires twice, but the main “This hand would” totals counted only its final activation. The forecast executed both activations on its copy, then discarded the first activation's effects while adding totals. This could make a worthwhile hand look weaker than it was.

**Reproduction:** one-carat Perfect Strike and Guard, each with Chatoyance, against a durable creature; hand `[6, 6, 2, 3, 1]`. The old forecast said **17 damage / 6 Block** while the real rail delivered **34 damage / 12 Block**. A second case used Chatoyance Glimmer with `[6, 8, 8, 8, 8]`: its first activation raises the only qualifying die, so the second fizzles. The gem should still show its one successful activation.

**Change:** `sim/battle.gd::forecast` accumulates all successful activations for totals and reports the number that actually fired. It keeps one activation's tally for the socket's per-activation display. Forecasting still leaves the live battle unchanged.

**Evidence:** `forecast-before.log` records the two failing damage/Block assertions. `forecast-after.log` records **222 battle assertions, zero failures**, including the state-isolation and Glimmer cases. These changes do not alter actual combat damage or content balance.

## Fixed: No Bench could leave no legal way out of a landing

**Player-visible problem:** the daily modifier forbids resting, but descending still required taking a respite. With no raw stone to appraise, fewer than 10 pyrite and only the daily's fragile gems, the well cannot be used either. A player can reach a state where the only escape is abandoning the run. An empty purse also cannot pay the lift.

**Reproduction:** a valid daily LENT_RAIL state with NO_BENCH, on the first landing, with each player's haul empty and ore zero. All their starting gems are fragile. Rest is refused, the lens has no target, the well refuses the rail and has no payment, and `choose: descend` previously returned “take your respite first”. This is a targeted reachable-state fixture; the 16 natural daily sweeps did not encounter this depleted state.

**Change:** No Bench makes the landing's respite optional. It still forbids healing from the fire. The simulation accepts descent without a respite only for this modifier; the UI leaves the tunnel mouths available alongside the optional lens/well/lift. Opening the well's page pauses the tunnel interaction, and backing out restores it. The help text explains the option to continue. Normal landings retain their existing respite rule.

**Evidence:** `no-bench-before.log` records both party members being refused and the party failing to advance. After the fix, `test_economy-no-bench.log` records **560 assertions / zero failures**, and `test_descent-no-bench.log` records **1,463 / zero failures**. `no-bench-rendered.log` records **six real-renderer interaction checks / zero failures**: available mouths, explanatory copy, well page pause/return, emitted descent command and acceptance by the rules. The rendered state is [no-bench.png](../../build/review-2026-10-19/no-bench.png).

Also removed disconnected players from the vein's “Waiting for…” name list, following the multiplayer audit's finding.

## Fixed: stale balance assertions obscured useful checks

Fifteen baseline assertions across four suites disagreed with already-authored content changes. Their expected values and descriptions now follow the current pack; gameplay was not reverted to satisfy old tests:

- Good Strike reads `3 + 3 + 5 = 11`, not 10.
- Apex's cut thresholds are 20/18/16/14/12.
- Fury now deals 3 per rerolled die; the fixture's Flawless/missing-health result is 33.
- Perfect Mortar takes 50% of Block; Retain caps at 20.
- Miasma now applies one Poison per even die; the following Siphon heals five from nine total Poison.
- Fight rewards use the already-changed 20 base pyrite rather than 6, with elite winnings doubled but spending/refunds unchanged.
- Crush uses five times its matched value, producing 30 from a triple of sixes.
- Flawless Bastion's scaled Retain in this fixture reaches its existing cap of 20.

The updated suites passed: **Stones 2,931**, **Gem updates 194**, **Battle 222** after new regressions, and **Statuses 520** assertions. No content values were changed.

## Gameplay and UX proposals for review

1. **Make the cost of getting home visible before route selection.** In the 48 starter baseline runs, **26 runs** reached a desired extraction with insufficient pyrite. Example: seed 1200, no-reroll policy, depth 8, 52 HP, 32 pyrite, lift price 84; it subsequently fell. Avoiding fights also avoids their reliable income, while smithies and veins can look like the cautious choice. Consider a persistent “next lift / fare / shortfall” indicator and a first-run explanation that fights fund extraction. Review the economy before reducing prices or granting free extraction.
2. **Teach Ardor's unused-reroll tradeoff, and don't treat the automated win rates as balance conclusions.** The sampled “better hand” policy did not outperform never rerolling. It optimizes immediate damage/Block/healing and does not price Ardor's unused-reroll passive, all enemy traits, or future build changes. This makes the passive an onboarding opportunity and a limitation of automated balancing, not evidence that rerolls are broken.
3. **Review landing commitment clarity.** At normal landings, taking rest/appraisal/well closes the lift option; Warden halls have no lift. These rules work as authored, but a player accustomed to “rest, then leave” can make a costly mistake. Give the commitment a concise statement at the point of action, and show the next extraction opportunity on the chart. A layout redesign should be mocked up before implementation.
4. **Review late-mine build guidance.** Generic strong rails fell very early in the deepest mines. That is not a sufficient reason to nerf them: stolen gems, carat caps, Sturdy and other traits reward different builds. Consider naming the mine's dominant threats and one mechanical counter in the mine dossier, with links into the bestiary, so an experienced player can prepare rather than discover the requirement through repeated paid failures.
5. **Forecast scope remains worth a dedicated review.** Guaranteed activations now total correctly, but `forecast` intentionally uses a dry copy and does not execute the complete live replay queue. Probabilistic procs, queued replays and opal-created gems can still diverge from the displayed total. Consider explicit expected/guaranteed wording or a bounded simulation of guaranteed queued actions. This is more involved than the repeated-activation correction and is deferred.
6. **Documentation and in-game terminology need a wider consistency pass.** The README's current gameplay overview was corrected to describe preserved Resonance on a fizzle, the Birthstone rather than the retired Capstone, 35% maximum-health rests and the Hollow's current name. These corrections follow `DeepBattle.resolve_gem`, `resolve_birthstone`, `DeepDescent.rest_amount` and `content/deep_cut.json`; no gameplay rule was changed. Older dated design history and other help text still warrant a separate review.

## Console and limits

Every Godot process emitted Steam initialization diagnostics because no Steam client was running (`SteamAPI_IsSteamRunning`, missing install directory). This did not stop offline simulation or the rendered landing check. No `SCRIPT ERROR`, engine `ERROR`, or failed-progress event was emitted by either completed 162-run sweep. The specific intentional regression failures before fixes are retained in the named logs above.

Headless timing is **not** a GPU or interactive frame-rate benchmark. The baseline's 210.97 seconds and final sweep's 117.06 seconds include differing concurrent QA loads and a forecast-sampling bot. The difference is not a claimed performance improvement. Hardware rendering/performance conclusions belong to the separate rendered review.
